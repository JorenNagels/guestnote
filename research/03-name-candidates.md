# Name candidates

> ## ✅ DECIDED: **GUESTNOTE** (2026-08-10)
>
> `guestnote.be` + `withguestnote.com` + `getguestnote.com` free. `guestnote.com` and
> `.io` are squatted. Conflict-clean — no company found.
>
> **Why:** easy in both Dutch and English, no trademark clouds, and **event-neutral** —
> it still fits when venues pull the product toward parties and corporate events, which
> `Vownest` / `Ringlane` / anything `Vow-` would not.
>
> Everything below is the working history: ~600 domains checked and every rejected name
> with its reason. Kept so this isn't re-litigated.

Criteria: works in **Dutch and English**, easy for a Dutch speaker to say and spell,
`.com` preferred but `.be` acceptable. Joren checks domains himself.

Dutch phonetics to respect: avoid `th` (becomes t/d), avoid initial `w` (becomes v),
avoid `-tion`. Dutch speakers handle hard consonants, `oo`/`aa`, `-ly` and `-o` endings
well.

### Ruled out after checking

`Nuptira` (→ *nutria* the rodent; `-ira` reads pharmaceutical) · `Gatherly` (virtual events
co.) · `Invitely` (4 cos., incl. an RSVP app) · `Deckle` ([deckle.studio](https://deckle.studio/)

### On using Joren's own name — advised against

`joren.be` and `nagels.be` are taken. More importantly: **"Nagels" = *nails* in Dutch**
(reads as a nail salon), and a founder-named product undoes the separate-brand strategy —
competing planners won't resell a product personally tied to someone linked to S'e parti,
and white-label means the *planner's* brand should be the visible one. Keep the founder
name for the **legal entity** ("Nagels BV") and for `jorennagels.com` as a personal site.

### Key structural findings

- The entire pronounceable `.com` namespace under ~7 letters is registered.
- **English roots are 100% squatted; Dutch roots are wide open.** Squatters run English
  dictionary permutations. Every free `.com` found came from a Dutch or Latin root.
- `.be` is *not* meaningfully more available for common words — Belgians registered the
  dictionary too. It only helps for compounds and unusual words.
- Human first names are all gone on both `.com` and `.be`.

## The `.com` workaround (this is how you actually get one)

Every short pronounceable `.com` is squatted — I checked ~240. But prefix/suffix patterns
are wide open, and this is completely standard practice. **Joy did exactly this**:
`withjoy.com`.

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| `with-` | withclink.com · withwoven.com · withglow.com |
| `get-` | getclink.com · getwoven.com · getdeckle.com |
| `-hq` | clinkhq.com · lanternhq.com · guesthq.com |
| `use-` | useclink.com · useenvelop.com |
| `try-` / `join-` / `hey-` | tryclink.com · joinclink.com · heyclink.com |
| Non-`.com` TLDs | clink.be · clink.app · clink.events · clink.wedding · clink.co |

**Recommended combination:** own the `.be` for the local trust signal, plus a
`with-`/`get-` `.com` for the international/product face. Buy the bare `.com` off a
squatter later (€1–15k) once revenue justifies it.

## How I'd choose

1. **Clink** if you want one word that works in both languages, feels celebratory, and
   leaves the door open to events beyond weddings. My overall pick.
2. **Woven** or **Deckle** if you want to look premium to high-end planners.
3. **Headcount** if you're committing hard to B2B and want planners to instantly get it.
4. **Noor** (or another Dutch name) if you want maximum brand freedom and warmth.

Whatever you pick, check: `.be`, `.com`, the `with-`/`get-` variant, Instagram handle,
and an EUIPO trademark search for class 42 (software) and class 41 (event services).
